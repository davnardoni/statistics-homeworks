Stochastic Penetration Process — LLN and CLT

1. Introduction

We consider n systems and m independent attackers. Each attacker performs n penetration attempts, represented by independent and identically distributed random variables X₁, X₂, ..., Xₙ. We study three quantities:

Total score: Sₙ = Σ Xᵢ
Average score: Sₙ/n
Normalized total: Sₙ/√n

2. Probability Models

Bernoulli model: Xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}, with P(Xᵢ = 1) = p. A score of 1 represents a successful penetration, while 0 represents a failure.

E[Xᵢ] = p, Var(Xᵢ) = p(1 − p)

Rademacher model: Xᵢ ∈ {−1, +1}, with equal probabilities 1/2. This represents a symmetric random walk.

E[Xᵢ] = 0, Var(Xᵢ) = 1

4. Law of Large Numbers

The Law of Large Numbers states that the average score converges in probability to its expected value μ:

Sₙ/n → μ in probability
P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) → 0

In the Bernoulli model μ = p, while in the Rademacher model μ = 0. We estimate the deviation probability using ε = 0.1.

5. Variance and Central Limit Theorem

Since the increments are independent with variance σ², the three normalizations have different variances:

Var(Sₙ) = nσ²
Var(Sₙ/n) = σ²/n
Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ²

Thus, the total variance increases with n, the average variance approaches zero, and the normalized total maintains constant variance.

The Central Limit Theorem states that:

(Sₙ − nμ)/(σ√n) ⇒ N(0, 1)

For Rademacher increments, μ = 0 and σ = 1, so Sₙ/√n converges in distribution to N(0, 1). For Bernoulli increments, centering is necessary: Sₙ/√n has mean p√n and does not converge to N(0, 1) when p > 0.

6. Conclusions

Increasing n: the total score becomes more variable, while the average converges to its expected value, confirming the Law of Large Numbers. The square-root normalization preserves the variance and, in the Rademacher case, leads to the standard normal distribution as predicted by the Central Limit Theorem.

Increasing m: more attackers provide more independent observations, producing smoother histograms and more accurate empirical estimates. However, m does not change the probability distribution of an individual attacker.

The model assumes independent attempts with identical probabilities. Real penetration processes may also involve dependencies and different vulnerability levels.
